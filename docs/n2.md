# The install on n2

n2 is the node the three vision models stand on. This document states what is installed there, what it
measured, how the three models share the one card, and how the card is taken from bmo-arbiter. Every number
was taken on n2 at the time and commit named with it, on 2026-10-27.

## The node

| What | Value |
|---|---|
| Host | `node2.labuser.local`, Ubuntu 22.04, kernel 5.15 |
| Card | NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4070, 12,282 MiB, compute 8.9, driver 595.58.03 |
| CPU | Intel Core i9-9900KF, 8 cores, 16 threads, 3.6 GHz |
| RAM | 62 GiB |
| Disk | NVMe 916 GB, 354 GB free when the work began, 308 GB after the installs |
| Account | `labuser`; reached through the jake hub's control API, no ssh |

Every figure below is against the card's whole 11.9 GiB, less the 218 MiB the voice service keeps and the
300 MiB the card group reserves for itself.

## The engine

One engine serves both 27B models: llama.cpp under `~/eye/llama.cpp`, from release tag `v0.7.0`, commit
`620d2225cc436ab0b001e3200e590e3b286ef9ab`, version string `0.7.0-dev (build 1, commit 620d222)`.

It is built with `-DGGML_CUDA=ON -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release -DLLAMA_CURL=ON
-DCMAKE_CUDA_ARCHITECTURES=89`, against CUDA 12.5.82, gcc 11.4.0 and CMake 3.22.1. The build takes 18
minutes on the node's eight cores.

The read model does not run on llama.cpp. It runs in its own Python runtime, `~/eye/venv-ocr` (Python 3.10,
torch 2.5.1+cu124, transformers 4.46.3), the way its own card states. Speech runs in `~/eye/venv-whisper`
(faster-whisper, CTranslate2, CPU int8).

- **Tokens per frame are about 880** at the frame's own 1280×720, which is what fixes the frame budget:
  16 frames is 14,000 prompt tokens, and the context the card can hold at this configuration is 32,768.
  The 62 tok/s difference between the two videos' prefills is their transcripts, not their frames.
- **Frames were taken as one frame every 4 seconds for the minute and one every 43 seconds for the
  eleven**, since both were held to the same 16. What that misses in the long video is not measured; the
  answer was still right about the subject.
- **The speech is read apart from the frames** and put in the prompt: faster-whisper large-v3, int8, on the
  node's CPU, took 81 s for the 66 s clip and 537 s for the 11 min 32 s one, about one minute of work per
  minute and a half of video. Its transcript of the news clip wrote 안세영 as 안세용.
- **The thinking turn is off.** With `enable_thinking` left on, the model spent its whole 400-token answer
  on its reasoning and answered nothing; the run that produced the answers above asks with it off.

## Sharing one card

The three models do not stand on the card together, and the service never tries to.

| What | Measured |
|---|---|
| Unloading a model | under 2 s: one process exit frees the card |
| Loading a 27B | 4.4 s warm, 62.3 s cold (NVMe page cache empty) |
| Loading the OCR model | 5.4 s |
| A 27B beside the OCR model | impossible: 9.7 GiB + 7.0 GiB against 11.9 GiB |
| eye giving the card up | 2.6 s from the ticket to the card under 1 GiB |
| gguf-llm coming back | 6.0 s from the release to its `/health` and its 9.5 GiB |

A swap costs what a load costs, and a warm load is fourteen times cheaper than a cold one. Keeping the
last model resident is paid by the other occupant: while eye rests on the card, the translator stays off
it, so a long keep time is a long absence for the translator. The service keeps its model for ten minutes
by default and gives the card up at the first ask.

## The card

The card is taken from bmo-arbiter as the tier-1 occupant `eye`; no other service is stopped by hand.

The declaration stands in the repository's root, `eye.card.toml`:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `service` | `eye` |
| `card_mib` | 11400 |
| `priority` | 60 |
| `tier` | 1 |
| `spread` | `one` |
| `alone` | false |
| `[turn]` | `yield_after_s = 0`, `can_pause = false` |

Two changes are the operator's, because one is the rules file and one is the master's own configuration:

- the card rules' `occupants` list gains `eye` at tier 1, beside `voice`, `song`, `lab-arena` and
  `gguf-llm`:

```
- name: eye
  tier: 1
```

- the master's `CARD_DECLARATIONS` gains this repository's root, so the master reads `eye.card.toml`, and
  the master is restarted to read it.

Until those stand, the declaration's own `tier = 1` is what the master uses, which is the same rank.

**Verified on the node, 2026-10-27.** With `gguf-llm` holding the card and `eye` asking for a turn, the
master said `gguf-llm stopped to yield the card: eye's turn holds the card` 2.6 s later, and the card fell
from 10,379 MiB to under 1 GiB. When the turn was given back the master said `gguf-llm started again: the
card is its turn` and the translator answered `/health` 6.0 s after the release. Both occupants are tier 1,
so the same-rank yielding of the arbiter's rules is what moved the card.

## What is not measured here

- **Two looks at once.** Everything above is one look at a time.
- **A real office scan.** The samples are clean Korean documents this work made; folded pages, handwriting,
  stamps and phone photos are not in the set.
- **The 35B-A3B alternative.** Its weights are on the node and it was not run; it is the balance candidate
  if `see`'s speed becomes the problem.
- **The hosted rungs.** OpenRouter's key is present but its spending limit is zero, so every call answers
  403; Gemini has no key on this machine. Neither was measured. Claude and Codex take an image themselves,
  which is the chain's last rung and needs no key.
